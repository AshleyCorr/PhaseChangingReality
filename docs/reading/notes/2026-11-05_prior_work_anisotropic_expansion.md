## What came closest (abstracts only unless marked)

- **Migkas et al. 2020**, "Probing cosmic isotropy with a new X-ray galaxy cluster sample through the L_X–T scaling
  relation", A&A 636, A15. 313 X-ray clusters; the X-ray luminosity–temperature relation varies across the sky, read as
  an apparent variation of the Hubble constant with direction, about 4σ in the preprint's abstract toward Galactic
  (l, b) about (280°, −20°), about 5σ in a joint analysis in the published version; a 2021 follow-up by the same group
  (A&A 649, A151) enlarges the sample. Alternatives named by the authors: bulk flows, unaccounted X-ray absorption. A
  claim at modest significance, disputed; not a consensus result.
- **Colin, Mohayaee, Rameez and Sarkar 2019**, "Evidence for anisotropy of cosmic acceleration", A&A 631, L13. A
  maximum-likelihood fit to the JLA supernova catalogue finds a dipole in the deceleration parameter aligned with the
  CMB dipole and falling with redshift; isotropy rejected at 3.9σ; the monopole (the isotropic acceleration) consistent
  with zero at 1.4σ. Widely cited and widely contested (the treatment of the supernova frame is the usual objection);
  not a consensus result.
- **The Hubble tension**: Riess et al. 2022 (SH0ES), H₀ = 73.04 ± 1.04 km/s/Mpc from Cepheid-calibrated supernovae;
  Planck 2018, 67.4 ± 0.5 from the cosmic microwave background under ΛCDM; the gap usually quoted at about 5σ; the
  Chicago-Carnegie Hubble Program's 2024 status report argues for about 3σ once a supernova-calibration systematic is
  added. This is a measured disagreement between two methods, not a measured direction.

## Verdict

**FOUND** (as targets): the sky has been searched for a preferred direction in the expansion and two groups claim one
at modest significance, both disputed; the Hubble tension is measured. None of this is a confirmation of anything in
this programme (rule 13); they are the numbers a prediction of an uneven expansion would have to face, with their
directions and significances.

What would change the verdict: reading the Migkas and Colin papers in full, and the rebuttals (for Colin et al., the
responses on the supernova rest frame), before any sheet quotes a direction as a target.
