# Reading note: like twists repelling at short range, in supercoiled DNA and in the Möbius band (5 November 2026)

**Why read:** her instruction of 5 November ("The dna and mobious band are exactly analogous please read up and
incorporate"), after the twist-energy brief (`docs/design/twist_energies_2026-11-04.md`) named both as the nearest
published pictures of a force between like twists that grows as they are pressed together. She is not after the
billion now; the aim is the list of factors a store's energy has. **Who read:** an assistant session, 5 November 2026.
What was read in full is marked; the rest is abstract or news coverage only.

## 1. Supercoiled DNA: the "standard model" of a plectoneme

**Source read in full (accepted manuscript):** S. Neukirch and J. F. Marko, "Analytical description of extension,
torque and supercoiling radius of a stretched twisted DNA", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 106, 138104 (2011),
doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.106.138104 (the CHORUS accepted manuscript, 9 pages). A later paper by other authors
(arXiv:1606.00408) says the 2011 free energy is approximate and its agreement with experiment only qualitative;
that is enough for a form, which is all we take.

The free energy of the plectonemic part, their Eq. (3), per length of plectoneme:

    ½ C τ_p²  +  ½ A sin⁴α / r²  +  U(r)

- **Twist** ½ C τ²: C the torsional rigidity (94 nm as a persistence length in their fits), τ the excess twist per
  length. Quadratic: the linear rod. A full twist against a half on the same length is 4 to 1 here too.
- **Bending** ½ A sin⁴α / r²: A the bending rigidity (44 to 47 nm), r the superhelical radius, α the superhelical
  angle. The two strands wrap round each other; bending costs more the tighter the wrap.
- **Repulsion between the two strands** U(r) = k_B T ν² L_B K₀(2 κ_D r), their Eq. (2): the Debye-Hückel interaction of
  two charged cylinders whose axes are 2r apart, ν the effective line charge, L_B the Bjerrum length (0.7 nm), κ_D⁻¹
  the Debye screening length set by the salt. K₀ is the modified Bessel function: it falls off exponentially when
  the strands are farther apart than the screening length and **rises without bound, as a logarithm, as the
  strands are pressed together**. This is the published form of her "the force pushing the twists apart is stronger
  at closer distances and this is the closest possible distance": a repulsion that grows as the separation shrinks,
  with the growth set by a screening length, not by the twist itself.
- **The constraint** ΔLk = ΔTw + Wr, their Eq. (4): the linking number is shared between twist and writhe (her coil);
  the molecule chooses the radius and angle by balancing bending and twist against the repulsion (their Eqs. 5, 6),
  and the result does not depend on how much linking is added, only how much length goes into the plectoneme.

**What it gives her picture.** Three separate factors, each with its own constant: a twist stiffness, a bending
stiffness, and a repulsion between the two halves that depends on how close they sit, with a screening length. In
DNA the repulsion is electric; what plays its part between two same-hand half-twist loops is hers to name. The
energy of a store would then be: twist of the joined full twist + bending of the two halves wrapped together +
U at the closest separation. A billion is not in this form: K₀ grows only as a logarithm, so a very large store
energy would need the separation to be a very small fraction of the screening length. She has said not to worry
about the billion now; recorded so that it is not forgotten.

**Not read:** J. Ubbink and T. Odijk, "Electrostatic-undulatory theory of plectonemically supercoiled DNA",
*Biophys. J.* 76, 2502 (1999) (the PMC page was blocked to the fetch); it adds an entropic undulation term to the
repulsion. Marko and Siggia 1995 on thermal fluctuations of plectonemes: not read, cited by the above.

## 2. The Möbius band

**Source: abstract and the authors' page, not the full text.** E. L. Starostin and G. H. M. van der Heijden, "The
shape of a Möbius strip", *Nature Materials* 6, 563–567 (2007), doi:10.1038/nmat1929. The problem was posed by
Sadowsky (1930) and extended to finite width by Wunderlich.

- The strip is modelled as an inextensible, unshearable sheet (an isometric embedding of a rectangle), so its
  equilibrium shape is a **developable surface** and its energy is **entirely bending**: with Hooke's law for
  bending, the energy is proportional to the integral over the surface of the square of the one non-zero principal
  curvature. There is no twist term at all: a Möbius band stores its half twist as bending.
- The shape is **independent of the material**, provided it is stiff enough for gravity not to matter: the same
  point the brief made for the rod (the material cancels in a ratio of like shapes).
- The shape depends on the **width-to-length ratio** of the rectangle; as the strip gets wider, the solutions form
  creases bounding nearly flat triangular regions, the energy localising at the creases (the authors connect this
  to crumpling and draping).

**What it gives her picture.** A Klein bottle is two Möbius bands glued along their edges (general knowledge). By
this picture a store's energy is bending, localised, and set by the band's width against its length, with the
material cancelling. The toy's objects have a length (√2) and no width; the width-to-length ratio is a new
quantity the record does not have. Her "a shorter loop holding a half twist is bent tighter" is this picture's
language (bending, not twist).

## 3. What the two together say for the brief (ours, from the reading)

The factors a store's energy has, each published in its own setting: (a) a twist stiffness (the rod; DNA's C);
(b) a bending stiffness (DNA's A; the Möbius band's only term); (c) a repulsion between the two halves that grows as
they are pressed together, with a screening length (DNA's U(r)); (d) a width-to-length ratio (the Möbius band).
Constants: four at most, all free until one is calibrated. The brief's choice 1 is amended accordingly. None of
this is a result; it is the list of factors she asked for.
