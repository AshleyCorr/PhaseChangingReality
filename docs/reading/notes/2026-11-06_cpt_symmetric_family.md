# Reading note: the family of "pair" cosmologies, in plain words, and what to borrow (6 November 2026)

**Why read:** her words of 6 November ("should we read up on similar models to see if we can borrow one ideally, im
struggling with these ideas"), after the CPT-symmetric universe was named as the nearest published shape of her rule
that the two feet of one seed carry opposite kinds and the books balance. **Who read:** an assistant session,
6 November 2026, from abstracts, the arXiv listings and press only; nothing below was read in full, and every
statement is general knowledge, to verify at the source before it is cited anywhere public. **What she is after:** a
published model to borrow, not novelty.

The one idea the whole family shares, in a sentence: **a surplus of matter here does not need a law that prefers
matter; it needs a boundary, with the opposite surplus on the other side, so that the two sides together carry
nothing.** Her boundary is the edge of the loose remnant; her two sides are the two feet.

## The models, one by one

1. **Sakharov's three conditions (1967).** Not a model but the checklist every surplus must pass: (i) a process
   that changes the count of matter minus antimatter; (ii) a preference between the kinds (C and CP violation);
   (iii) a moment out of equilibrium, so that the preference is not undone. In her picture the burp is (iii), the
   making and cancelling of pairs is (i), and (ii) is replaced by position: which side of the boundary. Sakharov
   also wrote of a universe with branches running with opposite arrows of time, the seed of the next entry
   (the 2026 review arXiv:2609.26834 says so; to verify).

2. **The CPT-symmetric universe** (Boyle, Finn and Turok, Phys. Rev. Lett. 121, 251301, 2018; arXiv:1803.08928
   and 1803.08930). In plain words: the bang is a mirror. On the far side of it sits the image of our universe
   with charge flipped, space mirrored and time run backward: an antimatter universe. Neither side is "first"; the
   pair together is exactly symmetric, so nothing had to prefer matter. The model also gives a dark-matter
   candidate (a very heavy stable neutrino, about five hundred million proton masses; press figure, to verify).
   **Borrow:** the rule that the two sides of a boundary are mirror images in kind, with the books balanced
   across it. That is the published form of her rule "the first pair made across the boundary sets the outside
   foot's kind, and everything after follows it": the inside foot carries the reverse of the outside foot.
   **Differs:** one bang, not many burps; the partner lies before the bang in time, not beside it in space; and
   the surplus *within* one side is not derived by the pair rule alone (to check in the paper).

3. **The two-sheeted universe** (Boyle and Turok, arXiv:2109.06204, 2021). The same picture carried further: a
   universe of radiation and vacuum energy, followed through its mathematics, has two sheets, swapped by a
   symmetry, joined at the bang, with conformal time running in a loop from one onto the other. **Borrow:** "two
   sheets joined at a boundary, a symmetry swapping them" is her two feet of one seed, said in a published way.
   **Differs:** no black hole, no repeat.

4. **The Hawking pair** (Hawking 1975; the tunnelling reading, Parikh and Wilczek 2000). A pair made at the
   horizon; one falls in, one escapes; the inside partner carries the reverse of whatever conserved charge the
   outside one carries, so the hole's books balance (Gibbons 1975 for charge). **Borrow:** a boundary that makes
   pairs with one on each side. **Differs:** the radiation outside is thermal and of both kinds equally for a
   neutral hole; it gives a pair per event, not a daughter.

5. **Torsion baryogenesis in a black-hole bounce** (Poplawski, Phys. Rev. D 83, 084033, 2011; arXiv:1101.4012).
   Inside a black hole the spin of matter twists spacetime (torsion); at the extreme density of the bounce the
   twist makes matter and antimatter sit at different energies, so decays favour one; the antimatter goes to a
   hidden sector that is the dark matter; the total stays zero. **Borrow:** twist deciding the kind inside a black
   hole, with the total zero and the other kind hidden. This is the closest machinery to hers. **Differs:** one
   sign per bounce, set by the torsion coupling, no boundary and no alternation.

6. **Matter and antimatter regions in one universe** (Alfven and Klein, 1960s). Our universe holds both kinds in
   separate regions. Measured against: the seams would glow in gamma rays, and the sky shows none, so any
   antimatter region must be larger than the part we can see (Cohen, De Rujula and Glashow, Astrophys. J. 495,
   539, 1998). Her decision of 6 November, "one daughter is one foot", keeps her clear of this.

7. **Alternating cycles** (Hajdukovic, 2014 onward). Cycles alternately of matter and antimatter. Rests on
   antimatter falling up; ALPHA-g at CERN measured antihydrogen falling down in 2023 (Anderson et al., Nature
   621, 716). Her alternation needs no antigravity (the prior-work note of the same day).

## The recommendation (ours)

Borrow entry 2, stated for her picture in one sentence: **the boundary is a mirror; whatever the outside foot
carries, the inside foot carries the reverse, and the pair together carries nothing.** Entry 5 is the one to read
first in full, because it is twist deciding the kind inside a black hole, and because its "other kind hidden as
dark matter" may bear on her open question of which carries the dark matter at birth (STATE, owed 5). Entries 2
and 3 are to be read for the exact statement of the mirror rule before it is written into the claim.

## On her dark-matter collar, the same day

Her correction: dark matter does not shed energy, it captures it; gravity's energy becomes the binding of two
like-handed particles into one Klein store heavier than its parts; gravity is one-directional so it keeps adding.
Published neighbours and facts (general knowledge, to verify):

- A bound state that is *heavier* than its parts is an excited state; it holds together only behind a barrier.
  Ordinary bound states are lighter (the deuteron is 2.2 MeV lighter than a proton and a neutron). Heavier-than-
  parts states that last do exist in nuclei (isomers such as tantalum-180m, which has lasted the age of the
  Earth behind its barrier). Her store "cannot cancel" (the barrier B) is that shape. Measurement: the barrier
  must be real and its height sets how long the store lasts.
- Energy from gravity has a cap *outside* the horizon: a particle falling toward a black hole can hand over to the
  outside at most a fraction of its rest mass before it crosses (about 6 % at the last stable orbit of a
  non-spinning hole, up to about 42 % for a maximally spinning one). Her pushback of the same morning: the collar
  sits inside. Inside, the classical theory puts no such cap on the energy two falling particles meet with (the
  Banados, Silk and West effect of 2009 is the best-known form); so a store made inside may be as heavy as the
  meeting pays for. The cap is withdrawn for the inside; what remains is that no measurement reaches inside, so
  the claim is checked only by what comes out. Gravity pays for the store; it does not bind it (the equivalence
  principle: the other forces act inside as outside).
- Capturing kinetic energy into a heavier state is an *inelastic, endothermic self-interaction* of dark matter;
  that family exists (Blennow, Clementz and Herrero-Garcia, arXiv:1612.06681, 2017, and the "inelastic dark
  matter" of Tucker-Smith and Weiner 2001). Any such interaction is limited by the Bullet Cluster: the two halos
  passed through each other with little drag, giving a cross-section per mass below about 1 cm^2/g (Randall et
  al. 2008; Robertson, Massey and Eke 2017 argue the bound is looser than claimed). So "layering by combining"
  is allowed, published, and bounded; the bound is what a collar model must stay under. Measurement.
