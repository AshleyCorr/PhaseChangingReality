## 1. Current definitions (from `HYPOTHESIS.md`, Part 2, and `twist_curl_rules_current.md`; unchanged here)

- **Mass** (hers, Update 139, 148): counts the looped diagonals, twisted or not; the unit is the electron, three white
  thirds, 0.511 MeV, so one looped diagonal is 0.170 MeV (exact). Mass has parts (twist, spin, charge, loop) in their
  own units; MeV is the measured total.
- **Twist** (hers): only where there is curl; matter twist in signed thirds on the √6 box diagonals, dark twist in
  signed halves on the √2 face diagonals; in two dimensions halves only.
- **The dark particle** (hers): a one-sided loop of surplus curl on the face diagonals, one loop holding a half twist;
  the heaviest **per length**, not necessarily per particle (her correction of 4 November).
- **The Klein store** (hers, 4 November): two same-hand half-twist one-sided loops joined into one full twist on a √2
  diagonal; a yes-or-no flag in the toy; cannot move, cannot cancel.
- **Her ratio claim** (hers, 4 November, verbatim in the notebook): "The binding energy held when two same-hand
  half-twist one-sided loops join into one full twist on a √2 diagonal, a Klein store, is about a billion times the
  energy of one particle of dark matter (1/2 twist on 1 loop)."
- **Her count claim** (hers, 4 November refinements): matter to antimatter is one to one by rule; the one-in-a-billion
  is a count of stores against pairs. She is not aiming at 5.36 or at a billionth: "there are other factors at play."
- **A shorter loop holding a half twist is bent tighter** than a longer loop holding a whole twist, so it holds more
  energy per loop (hers); the elastic-rod form is a stand-in of ours, not her rule.
- **Not adopted** (`WITHDRAWN.md`): the twist term κ(1 − cos 2πτ); the diagonal ratios as what *sets* the energy split;
  mass as one additive unit.
- **In the toy today** (exact): the only energy is the curling cost; one full curl is one unit; a pair costs two; a
  store holds its partner's unit and, under the ledger price, B + 4(λ − 1). No MeV anywhere.

## 3. What the existing rules already say (exact; ran before this brief)

- **Count rule** (hers): dark particle 0.170 MeV; store 0.170 MeV (0.341 if both diagonals stay looped). Store to
  dark **1 or 2**. Every loop weighs the same per length, so "heaviest per length" needs a twist part the count does
  not have.
- **Rod rule** (ours, stand-in; the twist energy of a uniformly twisted elastic rod, κτ²/L, general knowledge, to
  verify): store to dark **exactly 4** (a full twist against a half on the same length), whatever κ. Per unit length
  the dark particle's twist energy is **6.75 times the electron's**, so the rod form gives her "heaviest per length"
  with nothing fitted; the proton's twist energy per length is negligible, so a proton's mass is all count.
- **The toy as built** (exact): store to dark = 1 + B + 4(λ − 1) under the ledger price: **1.2 to 26** over the
  settings run.
- **Her billion** needs a binding β ≈ 10⁹ × E_dark: with the dark particle at the count rule's 0.170 MeV, **170 TeV**
  per store. No rule in the record gives a number within eight orders of this. The binding is a constant of its own
  (choice 1), not something the count or the rod form produces.
- **Her two billions cancel.** If stores are one in a billion of the pairs and each holds a billion dark energies, the
  stores' total energy equals N_pairs × E_dark exactly, the pairs' own weight as dark particles, whatever E_dark is.
  Arithmetic of her two statements together; whether she means them together is hers.
- **Reported as prominently as the convenient facts (rule 7): on the sheet, stores are not one in a billion of the
  pairs.** T105 (her clock, B = 0) at every side from 8 to 24: once stores form (λ ≥ 1.25) the stores made are
  **0.18 to 0.26 of the pairs made**, and the frozen single curls (dark particles) 0.07 to 0.19 of the pairs. The
  sheet's count is of order one in five, not one in a billion. Either the billionth lives somewhere the sheet is not
  (the inner shell, the opened tube, three directions), or it is the energy ratio and not the count, or it is not a
  number of this toy. Which, is hers; the exact test that tells the first two apart is the same moves on the opened
  tube six around (T106 when registered).
- **Planck's dark-to-ordinary mass ratio** (5.36, general knowledge, to verify) at a 0.170 MeV dark particle would
  need about 29,500 dark particles per proton. A target, not a result.

## Her answers of 5 November 2026 (verbatim in `sandbox/NOTEBOOK.md` the same day), as read

- **Choice 1**: the linear rod's 4 is too low. A store contains two dark particles and she expects more than two
  more to hold it together; the force between like twists grows at close range. A stiffening form (E ∝ τ^p gives
  2^p; a billion needs p ≈ 30) or a binding β; the form is hers, not given yet. A material choice does not change
  the 4 (the material cancels in a ratio of like shapes; only bend-to-twist, 1 + Poisson's ratio, is material).
- **Choices 2 and 4**: the dark particle's 0.170 MeV is the count rule's number and is not confirmed; it is not a
  calibration of the dark particle.
- **Choice 7 and section 3's one-in-five**: the billion lives where the sheet is not: in the inner shell, as the
  boundary row of a tessellated shell of stores, circumference over area (the T97 form, 1/R), about 2 × 10⁹ links
  across the radius. T106 on the tube is a test of the room stand-in, not of her shell; her shell needs a drift of
  stores (or halves) toward the center under gravity, which conflicts with "a store cannot move" and is hers to resolve.
- Choices 3, 5, 6: open.

## Amendment of 5 November 2026: the factors, from the reading she asked for

Her instruction (5 November, verbatim in the notebook): "Don't worry about the billion at this point, we are just
identifying the different factors at play. The dna and mobious band are exactly analogous please read up and
incorporate." Read: `docs/reading/notes/2026-11-05_twist_repulsion_dna_mobius.md`. Choice 1 is amended: a store's
energy has up to four published factors, each with its own constant, all free until calibrated:

| Factor | Published setting | Form | Constant |
|---|---|---|---|
| twist | the elastic rod; DNA's torsional rigidity | ½ C τ² per length | C |
| bending | DNA's bending rigidity; the Möbius band's only term | ½ A κ² per length (DNA: A sin⁴α / r²) | A |
| repulsion between the two halves | the Debye-Hückel interaction of two charged strands in a plectoneme | U(r) = u K₀(2r/ℓ), rising as a logarithm as the halves close | u, a screening length ℓ |
| width against length | the Möbius band's shape and energy localisation | not a formula in the sources read | the ratio |

What stays: the material cancels in any ratio of like shapes (the rod and the Möbius band both say so). What
changes: her "stronger at closer distance" now has a published form, the repulsion, and her "bent tighter" is the
bending term, not twist. A billion is not in these forms (the brief's section 3 stands); set aside at her word.
